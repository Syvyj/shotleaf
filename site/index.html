Shotleaf is one small app for two everyday jobs: showing someone what’s on your screen, and finding the thing you copied an hour ago. It runs on your Mac and keeps your data there.

Mark it up in seconds

Arrows, boxes, callouts, numbered steps, blur, a ruler and crop, each one key away. Then copy with ⌘C, save as PNG, JPEG, HEIC or PDF, pin it over your windows, or drag it straight into a chat.

Text from anything you can see

Backgrounds and watermarks

Put a shot on a gradient, remove the background in one click, sign it with text or your logo.

A watermark on a whole shoot

Sign many photos at once, each placed by hand if you like. Full quality and metadata stay; the originals aren’t touched.

Links that expire

Share a shot as a link that stops working after 1, 6 or 24 hours, served from your own server.

A clipboard that remembers

Press ⇧⌘V and the history opens by your pointer. Type a few letters, then click or press ↩: it pastes straight into the app you were in.

  • Text, links, colours, images and files; even words inside images are searchable
  • Passwords from password managers are never kept
  • ⌘K strips trackers from links, changes case, tidies JSON
  • Pick several and paste them together or one by one

Install

Shotleaf isn’t notarised by Apple yet, so macOS asks once before the first launch.

  1. Move Shotleaf to Applications and open it.
  2. If macOS says it can’t check the app, open System Settings › Privacy & Security and click Open Anyway.
  3. Allow Screen Recording for screenshots and Accessibility for pasting when the setup guide asks.

Needs macOS 15 or later on a Mac with Apple silicon.
